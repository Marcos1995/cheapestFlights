## Problem Statement

Google Flights / Skyscanner / Kayak show the obvious itinerary. Desvío exists to surface three other things:

1. **Tarifa error** — published fare far below the route's normal price. Lasts hours. Alert product, not search.
2. **Desvío legal** — extra stop, nearby airport, or self-transfer. You fly every segment. Cheaper than the simple ticket.
3. **Ciudad oculta** — want FCO, book BCN→RGS via FCO, get off in Rome. Contract of carriage violation.

Session 1 asked if this idea makes sense. It is a real category. It is not a Skyscanner clone.

Session 2 (this one): the BCN one-way mock prototype is already on `main`. Demand is still zero. The problem is no longer "what to build." It is "whether anyone cares."

## Demand Evidence

Still none beyond founder-as-user. The Barcelona–Rome / Burgos-via-Rome story is a workflow, not pull.

The prototype is the instrument. It has not been used as one. No interviews. No "would you book this." No one has been upset that it disappeared, because nobody outside this repo has it.

## Status Quo

Unchanged: people pick the top Google Flights result, or they already know Kiwi / Skiplagged / Secret Flying. Desvío is not in that loop yet.

## Target User & Narrowest Wedge

Still: a Barcelona leisure traveler, carry-on, 2–6 trips a year, will add 3+ hours to save €50–150.

The smallest version someone would pay for *this week* is not more software. It is the existing demo, shown to 10 real BCN flyers, with one question: would you book the **legal detour** if the price were real.

If fewer than 3 of 10 would book the legal detour (not hidden-city curiosity), the wedge is a toy. Stop or pivot. Do not add GDS to postpone that answer.

## Approaches Considered

### Approach A: Freeze and interview (minimal viable)

Summary: Leave the mock search as-is. Put a public URL in front of 10 BCN flyers. Write their answers. Decide keep / pivot / kill.

Effort: S  
Risk: Low  
Pros:
- Measures the thing the last design said to measure.
- Avoids paying for fare data to impress nobody.
Cons:
- Mock prices can be sniffed as fake in a call.
- No live inventory means "would you book" is still hypothetical.

Reuses: existing Next.js app, BCN→FCO/CDG templates.

### Approach B: Live fares now (ideal architecture next)

Summary: Wire Kiwi Tequila or Duffel so the savings are real, then interview.

Effort: M  
Risk: High  
Pros:
- Interviews stop bouncing off "this is demo data."
- Unlocks the live-phase 15%/€40 bar from the prior design.
Cons:
- Pays the hard cost (API, ToS, coverage gaps) before knowing if anyone wants the wedge.
- Classic stall: engineering instead of conversations.

### Approach C: Telegram error-fare bot (lateral)

Summary: Abandon search. Alert BCN/MAD/VLC when a fare is ≥50% below a median.

Effort: M  
Risk: Med  
Pros:
- Matches the "€1000 becomes €200" line.
- Distribution is already Telegram.
Cons:
- Ignores the Burgos-via-Rome quote, which is routing, not a glitch.
- Competes with Secret Flying / FlightGlitch on freshness.

Ruled out this session: C is a different product. B is the expensive delay.

## Recommended Approach

**A**, because the last assignment is undone and the code already can run a conversation. Live fares are the prize *after* 3 of 10 would book a legal detour.

Locked from session 1 (unchanged):

- One-way, BCN only.
- Destinations: FCO, CDG, LGW, AMS, LIS, ATH, BER, MXP, PRG, VIE. Full templates on the first five.
- One card per bucket. Mock catalog.
- Hidden-city templates only (FCO → ticket BCN–RGS via FCO).
- UI Spanish, code English.
- € saved vs simple bucket. Never claim "beats Google Flights" until a live source exists.

This session adds:

- Feature freeze until the 10-note exists.
- Interviews use **BCN→FCO only**. Other destinations exist in the picker; they are not this week's assignment. FCO is the canonical route. Hidden-city templates exist on five anchors in the catalog; they are out of scoring.
- Interview script (same every call). Before the demo: "Precios de ejemplo; quiero saber si el tipo de desvío te interesaría si el precio fuera real." Show results. Discuss **only** the Desvío legal card. Do not open ciudad oculta unless they ask. Point at the numbers on screen (saved € and extra hours), not a memorized €80 / 4h line. €80 / 4h is one example of the wedge (3+ hours, €50–150), not a second metric.
- Scoring Q4: **Yes = 1**, **No = 0**, **Only if live = 0**. Hidden-city reactions are notes only; they do not count.
- Kill bar: fewer than 3 **Yes** of 10 → do not add GDS. Pivot to alerts or stop within 48 hours of finishing the notes.
- Continue bar: ≥3 **Yes** → Approach B (live fares) is in scope.
- Demo blockers (only things that may be fixed during freeze): (a) public deploy, (b) BCN→FCO shows a legal-detour card on a phone, (c) screen-share does not break the layout. Everything else frozen.
- Mock-data escape hatch (narrow): only if ≥5 of 10 bounce citing fake prices **and** ≥2 said they would book if real. Then at most one live route (BCN→FCO), one API, ≤1 day. Not a general GDS project.

## The Assignment

**Deploy (before the first call):**

2. Confirm `npm test` and `npm run build` pass.
3. Deploy.
4. Open the public URL.
5. Search BCN→FCO and confirm the **Desvío legal** card renders on desktop and a phone.

**Run the script. One file per person:** `docs/interviews/note-NN.md`

```
date:
name_or_initials:
screener_ok: yes/no
q1_paid:
q2_worse_itinerary_if_save:
q3_used_kiwi_or_skiplagged:
q4_book_legal_detour: yes | no | only_if_live
```

Questions:
1. What did you pay on a recent BCN trip.
2. Looking at **this** Desvío legal card: extra hours and € saved on screen. Would a detour like that (you fly every segment) be acceptable.
3. Have you used Kiwi or Skiplagged.
4. Would you book this **desvío legal** if that price were real. Yes / no / only if live.

Do not add destinations, GDS, or error-fare alerts until the notes exist. Do not score hidden-city.
